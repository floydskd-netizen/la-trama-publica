Has Netanyahu become a major problem for Israel?

Israel faces real security threats. At the same time, Netanyahu's government faces prolonged war, growing diplomatic isolation and declining international confidence. The question is how much of that cost is unavoidable and how much reflects choices made by the government itself.

The image that opened the question

Netanyahu rose to defend Israel. Dozens of delegates walked out. What does that say?

On September 24, dozens of delegates left the UN General Assembly as Netanyahu began speaking. The episode does not establish why every delegate left, but it is a visible sign of the diplomatic pressure and criticism surrounding his government.

Reuters: the UN walkout ↗

First distinction

Netanyahu is not Israel. Criticism of his government is not criticism of Jews as a people.

Israel is a plural society with opposition parties, protests, media and elections. Responsibility for government decisions should remain with the officials and institutions that make them, not with Israelis or Jews collectively.

Security

Israel faces real threats. Does that make every response effective or justified?

No conclusion follows automatically. Hamas, Iran and Iran-backed armed groups pose documented threats to Israel. Government strategies still have to be examined by their consequences for security, civilian life, alliances and international legitimacy.

International standing

Israel can achieve military objectives while losing diplomatic support.

Pew's 2026 survey found unfavorable views of Israel across many of the 36 countries studied and low confidence in Netanyahu. In Argentina, 55% expressed an unfavorable view of Israel and 63% had little or no confidence in Netanyahu. Those figures document a trend; they do not by themselves prove what caused it.

Iran

Iran's nuclear program raises serious concerns. Does that settle the debate over war?

The IAEA has reported serious access, transparency and monitoring problems. That establishes a genuine security concern, but it does not by itself determine that every military escalation is necessary, proportionate or effective.

Reuters: IAEA report on Iran ↗

United States

Do U.S. and Israeli interests always coincide?

They are close allies, but allies can have different priorities. U.S. military assistance to Israel and direct American participation in the Iran war make scrutiny of costs, objectives and decision-making a legitimate issue for Americans as well as Israelis.

Trump

Netanyahu does not erase Trump's responsibility for U.S. decisions.

On September 22, Trump said at the UN that he could “annihilate” Iran if there were no agreement. That does not establish that nuclear use was decided. It does show why U.S. presidential rhetoric and decision-making must be examined independently of Netanyahu.

Reuters: Trump's UN speech ↗

Power and democracy

A leader is not the country.

Israel is not Netanyahu; the United States is not Trump; Argentina is not Milei. Democratic office is temporary. Institutions and citizens remain larger than the person holding power.

Final question

How much of Israel's isolation comes from external threats, and how much from its government's choices?

The evidence does not reduce that question to a single cause. It does make the question legitimate — particularly for people who want Israel to remain secure, democratic and internationally sustainable.
